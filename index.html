Where the cotton comes from

Every Harbourline tee starts as long-staple cotton grown in the Riverina, spun in Melbourne and knitted on the same circular machines we have used since 2011. Longer fibres mean fewer loose ends, which means a fabric that pills less and holds its shape through more washes than it has any right to.

We wash every garment before it leaves the factory. It costs us more and it means the tee you pull out of the bag is the same size as the tee you pull out of the dryer in two years. Most brands skip this step. You find out why the first time you wash a shirt and it comes back a size smaller.

None of this is complicated. It is just slower, and slower costs money, which is why most of the industry stopped doing it. We kept doing it because the alternative is selling people something that falls apart, and that turns out to be a worse business than it looks on a spreadsheet.

The dye house runs on recycled water. The offcuts go to a mill in Geelong that turns them into industrial felt. We are not going to pretend that makes us a sustainable company, because it does not. It makes us a company that wastes slightly less than it used to, which is a smaller claim and a truer one.

Questions we get a lot

How do the sizes run?

True to size, cut slightly longer in the body. If you are between sizes and want it fitted, size down. If you want it relaxed, stay where you are.

Will the colour fade?

Reactive dyes bond to the fibre rather than sitting on top of it, so the colour goes soft rather than patchy. Wash cold and it will outlast the shirt.

Do you ship outside Australia?

New Zealand only for now. Everywhere else is on the list and has been for a while.

What if it does not fit?

Send it back within thirty days. The satchel is in the box. You do not need to tell us why.

Are the socks really worth twenty-five dollars?

For five pairs, yes. Reinforced heel and toe, and the ribbing is knitted rather than elasticated, which is the bit that usually goes first.
